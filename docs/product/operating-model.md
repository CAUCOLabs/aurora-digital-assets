## 1. Purpose

This document defines the operating model concept for Aurora Digital Assets.

The goal is to address a critical question:

> How could a bank justify offering digital asset access when custody, compliance, tax reporting, AML monitoring, support, and external withdrawals create real operational cost?

Aurora Digital Assets should not be understood as a bank trying to compete directly with global crypto exchanges on trading volume, leverage, high-frequency activity, or the lowest possible execution fees.

Instead, the project explores a different model:

- controlled digital asset access;
- limited asset scope;
- custody clarity;
- user protection;
- reporting;
- security;
- trust;
- retention;
- regulated financial experience.

The operating model must be realistic about cost, risk, and complexity.

---

## 2. Strategic Question

Many retail users already access crypto assets through global exchanges.

These platforms often have:

- deep liquidity;
- low trading fees;
- fast execution;
- large asset selection;
- active trading tools;
- global scale.

A bank-integrated digital assets product should not try to copy this model.

The strategic question is different:

> Should banks leave digital asset access entirely to exchanges, or should they build safer infrastructure around it for their own customers?

Aurora Digital Assets argues that banks may have a role if the product is designed around safety, reporting, custody clarity, and responsible access.

---

## 3. What This Model Is Not

The Aurora Digital Assets operating model is not:

- a high-frequency trading platform;
- a derivatives platform;
- a futures platform;
- a leverage product;
- a meme-coin marketplace;
- a speculative exchange clone;
- a yield or staking product;
- a promise of financial returns;
- a way to compete with global exchanges only on price.

The product should avoid features that increase user risk or operational burden without clear strategic value.

---

## 4. Proposed Product Position

Aurora Digital Assets should be positioned as:

> A controlled, bank-integrated digital assets access layer focused on trust, custody clarity, reporting, and user protection.

The product should prioritize:

- BTC and ETH first;
- spot-only access;
- no leverage;
- no futures;
- no margin;
- no gamified trading;
- transparent fees and spreads;
- internal custodial wallet view;
- controlled external withdrawals;
- audit trail;
- tax reporting support;
- clear risk communication.

This positioning makes the product different from a crypto exchange.

The goal is not maximum trading activity.

The goal is safer participation.

---

## 5. Target Customer Segment

The first target user is not an advanced trader.

The first target user is a retail banking customer who:

- is curious about digital assets;
- wants limited exposure;
- values safety over trading tools;
- prefers a familiar banking environment;
- does not want to manage private keys immediately;
- needs clear reporting;
- wants to understand custody and risk;
- may later choose self-custody through an external withdrawal.

This customer is different from a professional trader or exchange-native crypto user.

The bank product should be designed for clarity, not speed or speculation.

## 6. Cost Drivers

A bank-integrated digital assets product has real cost drivers.

Important cost areas include:

### 6.1 Custody

Custody may require:

- secure wallet infrastructure;
- cold storage or custody partner integration;
- segregation of customer assets;
- operational controls;
- incident response;
- insurance or risk coverage considerations;
- custody reporting.

### 6.2 Compliance and AML

Digital asset activity may require:

- transaction monitoring;
- sanctions screening;
- wallet risk checks;
- external withdrawal review;
- suspicious activity escalation;
- audit logs;
- regulatory reporting;
- Travel Rule considerations where applicable.

### 6.3 Tax Reporting

Retail users need support for:

- purchase history;
- sale history;
- transfers;
- realized gain/loss concepts;
- annual summaries;
- exportable reports;
- user-facing disclaimers.

### 6.4 Customer Support

Support may be needed for:

- failed or delayed withdrawals;
- user confusion about custody;
- address and network mistakes;
- reporting questions;
- account restrictions;
- security incidents;
- suspected scams.

### 6.5 Security

Security cost may include:

- strong authentication flows;
- withdrawal delays;
- address whitelisting;
- account takeover detection;
- fraud monitoring;
- secure audit trails;
- internal access controls.

These costs are real and should not be ignored.

---

## 7. Margin Reality

A bank-integrated digital assets product may not be profitable if it depends only on small retail trading fees.

This is an important constraint.

If the product is designed as a low-fee exchange competitor, the bank may inherit high operational cost without enough trading volume to justify it.

Therefore, the product should not be evaluated only as a trading-fee business.

The value may come from a wider banking relationship.

Possible value sources:

- custody fee;
- transparent transaction fee;
- premium reporting features;
- customer retention;
- increased engagement with the bank;
- wealth management integration;
- financial planning integration;
- business customer reporting tools;
- safer onboarding for users who would otherwise use external exchanges;
- long-term digital asset infrastructure positioning.

The business case should combine product revenue, customer trust, retention, and strategic positioning.

## 8. Revenue Model Options

Aurora Digital Assets could support several possible revenue models.

### 8.1 Transparent Transaction Fee

A simple fee for buy/sell transactions.

Principles:

- visible before confirmation;
- no hidden spread;
- no gamified trading incentives;
- no incentive to overtrade.

### 8.2 Custody or Service Fee

A small custody or digital assets service fee may be possible if the bank provides:

- custody visibility;
- reporting;
- support;
- security controls;
- external withdrawal protection;
- annual summaries.

This should be clearly disclosed.

### 8.3 Reporting and Premium Services

Some users may value:

- enhanced tax reports;
- downloadable PDF/CSV files;
- audit evidence packages;
- accountant-ready export;
- portfolio history.

### 8.4 Wealth and Advisory Integration

For eligible customers, digital assets could become part of broader financial planning.

This should remain carefully separated from investment advice unless the bank is explicitly authorized to provide it.

### 8.5 Business and Professional Accounts

Future versions could support entrepreneurs, companies, or professional users who need:

- cleaner records;
- treasury reporting;
- audit evidence;
- controlled wallet access;
- policy-based approvals.

## 9. Risk Reduction by Product Design

The operating model should reduce risk through product constraints.

Recommended constraints:

- start with BTC and ETH only;
- spot-only access;
- no leverage;
- no derivatives;
- no short selling;
- no yield promises;
- no staking in early versions;
- no meme-coin discovery;
- no aggressive trading charts;
- no push notifications encouraging speculation;
- clear suitability and risk warnings;
- withdrawal review for external transfers;
- limits for new users;
- cooling period for new external addresses.

These constraints may reduce short-term revenue, but they support trust and regulatory alignment.

## 10. Operational Workflow

A simplified operational workflow could look like this:

```text
Customer enters Digital Assets module
        |
Reads risk and custody information
        |
Chooses Buy / Sell / Wallet / Withdrawal / Reports
        |
System applies product limits and security controls
        |
Transaction or request is recorded
        |
Audit event is created
        |
Reporting records are updated
        |
External withdrawal requests may enter review
```

For external withdrawals:

```text
User enters destination address
        |
System checks asset, network, amount, and address format
        |
Risk warning is shown
        |
Strong authentication is required
        |
Request enters pending review if needed
        |
Audit event is created
        |
Withdrawal is approved, rejected, delayed, or completed
```

## 11. Bank vs Exchange Comparison

Aurora Digital Assets should not claim that banks are always better than exchanges.

The difference is product position.

| Area | Global Exchange Model | Bank-Integrated Model |
|---|---|---|
| Main value | Liquidity and execution | Trust, reporting, controlled access |
| Asset selection | Broad | Limited |
| Trading tools | Advanced | Minimal |
| User protection | Varies | Product-level priority |
| Custody visibility | Often unclear to retail users | Explicitly explained |
| External withdrawals | Fast, user-controlled | Controlled and reviewed |
| Reporting | Often available but separate | Integrated with banking records |
| Business goal | Trading activity | Safer financial relationship |

This comparison helps clarify why the bank model should not copy the exchange model.

- Should custody be direct or partner-based?
- What transaction volume would justify operational costs?
- What fee model would be acceptable to retail users?

## 14. Strategic Conclusion

The strongest case for Aurora Digital Assets is not that banks can offer cheaper crypto trading than global exchanges.

The strongest case is that banks can offer a safer, clearer, more transparent, and more accountable digital assets experience for users who already trust their banking environment.

The model must be honest about cost.

Custody, AML, tax reporting, security, and support create operational complexity.

But this complexity is also where banks may create value.

If digital assets become a long-term part of financial life, banks may need to decide whether they want to remain outside that user journey or build a responsible version of it.

Aurora Digital Assets explores what that responsible version could look like.
